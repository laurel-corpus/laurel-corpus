# Editorial commentary

*Part of the [Laurel corpus](../README.md). The [method index](../METHOD.md) lists the other documents,
and terms are defined in the [glossary](glossary.md).*

**Seventy-six works carry editorial notes, 18,220 of them in all.** They come from the public domain
school and scholarly editions that printed them, and they are shown against the stanza to which they
belong.

## Where the notes come from

There are two sources. Some Gutenberg files carry their editor's notes inline, and those are parsed out
and keyed to the stanza they annotate. Others come from school editions scanned at the
[Internet Archive](https://archive.org/), whose text is OCR.

## Placing OCR notes on a translated text

The harder case is a note written against an original that the reader is reading in translation. Such
notes are keyed to the *original's* line numbers, which do not correspond to the English.

Each note is first placed at the English stanza that sits at the same proportional depth in the poem,
and then moved to a nearby stanza when the note's lemma carries a proper name that the stanza also
names. That second step is what makes the placement trustworthy rather than merely plausible: a note
about Dido lands on the stanza that says Dido.

It is an approximation, and it is described as one. A note placed by proportion alone may sit a stanza
or two from where its editor intended.

## A known gap

A small number of notes are keyed to sections that this corpus names differently (the same poem under a
different slug), and they do not currently appear. They are counted in the corpus audit rather than
quietly dropped, and they are not re-matched automatically, because a confident but wrong match would
attach an editor's note to the wrong poem, which is worse than showing none.
