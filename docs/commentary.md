# Editorial commentary

*Part of the [Laurel corpus](../README.md) — see the [method index](../METHOD.md). Terms are defined in
the [glossary](glossary.md).*

**76 works carry editorial notes: 18,220 in all.** They come from the public domain school and
scholarly editions that printed them, and are shown against the stanza they belong to.

## Where the notes come from

Two sources. Some Gutenberg files carry their editor's notes inline, and those are parsed out and keyed
to the stanza they annotate. Others come from school editions scanned at the
[Internet Archive](https://archive.org/), whose text is OCR.

## Placing OCR notes on a translated text

The harder case is a note written against an original that the reader is reading in translation. Those
notes are keyed to the *original's* line numbers, which do not correspond to the English.

Each note is first placed at the English stanza sitting at the same proportional depth in the poem,
then snapped to a nearby stanza when the note's lemma carries a proper name that stanza also names.
That second step is what makes the placement trustworthy rather than merely plausible: a note about
Dido lands on the stanza that says Dido.

It is an approximation, and is described as one. A note placed by proportion alone may sit a stanza or
two from where its editor intended.

## A known gap

A small number of notes are keyed to sections this corpus names differently — the same poem under a
different slug — and do not currently appear. They are counted in the corpus audit rather than quietly
dropped, and are not automatically re-matched: a confident but wrong match would attach an editor's
note to the wrong poem, which is worse than showing none.
